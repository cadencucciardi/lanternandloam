Winter Sowing for Beginners: Milk-Jug Seed Starting From the Solstice On

Winter sowing means planting seeds in a covered container, setting it outside in the cold, and letting the weather do the work. The seeds sit through freezes and thaws, then sprout when the days warm up. There are no grow lights, no heat mats and no windowsill full of seedlings. If you have a recycling bin, you can start today.

Why winter sowing works

Many plants drop seeds in fall and wait out winter. Cold, wet weather softens the seed coat and tells the seed it is not spring yet. A jug with a clear top acts like a tiny greenhouse: it holds moisture, lets light in and shelters the soil from heavy rain. Seedlings that sprout in it tend to be tougher than ones raised indoors, because they have lived outside from day one.

When to start

Most guides say the window opens after the winter solstice (around Dec 21) and runs into early spring. You do not need mild weather, since the jugs are meant to sit through the cold. Use your seed packet and your local last-frost date as the guide, and ask your local extension office if you are unsure. Dates vary a lot by region, so treat any date you read online as a rough starting point.

What you need

  • Clear or translucent plastic jugs (milk, water or juice), rinsed out
  • Fresh bagged potting mix, not soil from the garden bed
  • A knife or sturdy scissors, and a drill or hot nail for holes
  • Duct or packing tape
  • Seeds
  • A pencil or permanent marker, and plant labels

The 5 steps

1. Cut the jug. Cut around the middle, leaving a hinge of about an inch at the handle side so the top can fold back like a lid. 2. Add holes. Poke 4 to 6 drainage holes in the bottom so water does not pool. Poke a few holes in the top half too, for air and rain. 3. Fill with mix. Add 3 to 4 inches of damp potting mix. Damp, not soggy: it should hold together when squeezed. 4. Sow and label. Scatter or press in seeds at the depth the packet suggests. Write the plant name on a label inside the jug, and also on the outside, because marker fades in sun. 5. Tape and set out. Close the jug with tape, leave the cap off, and put it outside somewhere with sun that you can reach easily. Rain and snow will water it for you.

What to sow first

Start with seeds that handle cold. Packets that say "cold stratify," "direct sow in fall" or "hardy" are good signs.

  • Hardy perennials and many native wildflowers
  • Hardy herbs such as chives and parsley
  • Cold-tolerant greens such as kale, spinach and lettuce
  • Cool-season flowers that the packet says can handle frost

Save tender plants like tomatoes, peppers and basil for later. They need warmth, so sow them in spring instead, once nights are not freezing.

Caring for your jugs

  • Check moisture now and then. If the mix looks dry during a long dry spell, add some water.
  • Vent on warm days. When spring days climb well above freezing, open the lid so seedlings do not overheat.
  • Be patient. Cold-loving seeds may sprout in late winter or not until spring. Waiting is normal.
  • Harden off and transplant. Once seedlings have a few true leaves and frost danger has passed, plant them out in the garden.

Quick recap

Cut a jug, add holes, fill with damp potting mix, sow cold-hardy seeds, label, tape and set it outside. Check on it sometimes, and move seedlings to the garden when frost danger is past. That is all winter sowing is.
