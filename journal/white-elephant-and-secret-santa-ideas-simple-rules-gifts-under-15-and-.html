If you got asked to organize the office swap, the family exchange or the neighborhood party, you probably have three questions: which kind of swap, what to buy, and how to run it without an awkward silence. This guide answers all three in plain terms.

White elephant or Secret Santa: which one?

  • White elephant (also called Yankee swap or dirty Santa): everyone brings one wrapped gift. People take turns picking a gift from the pile or stealing an opened one. It is loud, funny and works well when guests barely know each other.
  • Secret Santa: everyone draws one name and buys for that person only. It is quieter and more personal, and it works well for coworkers, classrooms and families who can't all gather on one day.

Rule of thumb: if you want a game at a party, pick white elephant. If people will exchange gifts over several days or at a distance, pick Secret Santa.

White elephant rules that keep it fair

Rules vary a lot by family and office. Here is one simple version you can use as is.

  • Everyone brings one wrapped gift at or near the agreed budget. Nobody labels who it is from.
  • Draw numbers from a bowl. Number 1 goes first and opens any gift from the pile.
  • Each later player either opens a new gift or steals an opened one from someone else.
  • A gift can be stolen up to 2 or 3 times in total, then it is "frozen" and stays with its owner. Pick the number before you start.
  • Stealing back from the person who just stole from you is not allowed on the same turn.
  • Optional: when the last person finishes, player 1 can swap with anyone.

Say these out loud before the first gift is opened, and write them on a card for the gift table. Most arguments come from rules nobody stated.

Secret Santa: how to run it without a mess

  • Collect names a week or two ahead and draw them in private. A hat works. So do slips of paper folded in half.
  • Share the budget, the delivery date and any hard no's (allergies, no scented items, no alcohol).
  • Ask each person for three likes, one thing they would rather not get, and a size if clothing is allowed. A short questionnaire makes the buyer's life much easier.
  • Set a reveal date, and remind people the day before.
  • Have a plan for the person who forgets or drops out: one spare, labeled gift in your bag covers most gaps.

Gift ideas under $15

These work for either swap, because they suit nearly anyone.

  • A nice mug filled with tea bags, cocoa packets or a small candy
  • A candle or a small jar of hand cream (go unscented if you don't know the person)
  • A pack of good pens and a small notebook
  • A seed-starting kit or a small potted herb for someone who likes plants
  • A deck of cards or a small travel game
  • A pair of warm socks
  • A bar of good chocolate with a small bag of coffee beans

Gift ideas under $25

  • A paperback, a bookmark and a bag of tea for a reader
  • A cozy blanket-style throw or a pair of slippers
  • A coffee or tea sampler set
  • A mini garden tool set or a pair of gardening gloves
  • A board game that plays in 20 minutes
  • An insulated tumbler or travel mug
  • A basket with snacks and a candle, wrapped as one gift

White elephant gifts: funny, useful or "everyone wants to steal it"

The best white elephant gifts are the ones people fight over. Think a useful item with a fun wrapper: a nice throw blanket, a good-smelling candle, a snack basket or a small speaker. The worst: anything that is only a joke. People steal gifts they would use.

A 20-minute plan for the party

  • 10 minutes before: gifts on a table, number bowl ready, rules card visible.
  • Start: read the rules out loud in under a minute.
  • During: one person keeps the list of who has which gift. Take photos.
  • End: let people trade gifts quietly if they want to, as long as both agree.

Common problems and quick fixes

  • A gift is far over or under the budget. Say the number in the invitation, and bring one backup gift for anyone who forgets.
  • Someone doesn't want to play. Make it optional and let them watch, or draw a name for them.
  • Too many people steal the same gift. Use the "frozen after 3 steals" rule and tell everyone upfront.
  • Remote coworkers. Switch to Secret Santa and mail the gifts, or use gift cards.

Whichever you choose, set the budget, say the rules out loud and keep the party short. People remember if it was fun, not whether the gift was perfect.
