How to Run a White Elephant or Secret Santa Swap (Rules, Price Tiers, Gift Ideas)

Gift swaps are fun when the host sets clear rules early, and awkward when nobody knows the budget or how stealing works. This guide covers how to pick a format, set a price limit, run the game, and what to buy at each price tier.

White elephant or Secret Santa: which one?

The two are often mixed up, so choose on purpose.

  • Secret Santa: Everyone draws one name and buys one gift for that person. It's private and thoughtful, and it works best when people know each other well, like a family or a small team. Gifts can be personal.
  • White elephant: Everyone brings one wrapped gift and takes turns picking or stealing. It's loud, funny and good for big or mixed groups, like an office party. Gifts should suit anyone.

Rule of thumb: under about 8 people who know each other, pick Secret Santa. A bigger or mixed group usually does better with white elephant.

Set the price limit first

Announce the budget in the invitation, not at the party. Most groups land in one of these tiers:

  • $10-15: Best for large offices and casual groups. Think printables, a small treat, a cozy item or a candle.
  • $20-25: A good default for friends and extended family. You can get a mug, a book or a nice tea or cocoa set.
  • $30-50: For close friends and family, where people expect something more personal.

Say whether the limit includes tax and shipping. Also decide if handmade or printable gifts are welcome. They often are.

How to run a white elephant game

1. Number the guests. Each person draws a number from a bowl. That sets the turn order. 2. Number 1 opens first. They pick any wrapped gift and open it. 3. Later players choose: they can open a new gift or steal an unwrapped one. 4. Set a steal limit. A common rule is that a gift can be stolen up to 2 or 3 times, and then it is locked. You can also ban stealing straight back. 5. Let number 1 go again at the end. They can swap with any gift, which makes up for going first.

Write the rules on a card or poster and put it where people can see it. It saves a lot of arguing.

How to run a Secret Santa

1. Collect names a few weeks ahead. Use a hat, a draw app or folded slips. 2. Ask for a short wish list. Three likes and one thing they don't want makes shopping much easier. 3. Set a reveal date. Pick one day for giving the gifts so nobody is left out. 4. Handle drawing yourself. Have a plan for anyone who gets their own name, and redraw if so.

Gift ideas by price tier

Keep in mind that white elephant gifts should suit anyone. Secret Santa gifts can match the person.

Around $10-15: easy and low cost

  • A printable set of Christmas gift tags to label every present. They also make a handy extra for the host.
  • A Kids Christmas Activity Pack for a swap that includes children, or for a friend who is a parent. It's an instant download, so there's no shipping.

Around $20-30: the sweet spot

  • A holiday mug, like the Merry & Booked mug for a reader, or the Warm Mug, Merry Sips mug for anyone who loves cocoa. Fill it with tea bags or hot chocolate mix.
  • The Garden Club mug for a gardener. Pair it with a pack of seeds.
  • The Just One More Chapter ornament for a book lover who has a tree.

Around $30-50: closer friends and family

  • Put a mug together with a book, tea and a candle in a small basket.
  • A cozy sweatshirt, such as the Just One More Chapter sweatshirt, is a good fit for a close friend who reads.
  • The Cozy Holiday Planner is a useful printable for the organized host who is planning the whole season.

Printed items are made to order, so allow processing and shipping time. If your swap is close, choose the printable gifts that download instantly.

Host tips that save the day

  • Ask guests to wrap everything the same way, so nobody can guess whose gift is whose.
  • Keep a few spare wrapped gifts for guests who forget.
  • Have a few backup rules for stolen gifts, and say that the host's decision is final.
  • For remote teams, set the budget and ship dates early.
  • Keep it kind. Remind people to pick something they'd enjoy getting, not a joke gift that will end up in the bin.

A clear price limit, a short rule card and an easy reveal date are most of what makes a swap go well. Pick your format, choose your tier, and enjoy the evening.
