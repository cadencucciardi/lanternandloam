Shopping for a gardener is easier than it looks. Most of them don't need another tool. They want something that feels like them: a little dirt-under-the-nails humor, a cup to hold while they plan next spring's beds, a bag that carries seed packets and library books alike. Here is how to choose, plus the Garden Club designs we make at Lantern & Loam.

How to choose a gift for a gardener

Start with how they spend their days.

  • The morning-coffee-in-the-yard gardener: a mug is the safest, most used gift.
  • The farmers-market and seed-swap gardener: a sturdy canvas tote.
  • The always-in-the-garden gardener: a tee for warm days, a sweatshirt for cold-weather puttering.
  • The winter gardener (planning, seed catalogs, houseplants): cozy layers and a mug for catalog season.

Also think about the person's taste. If they like understated things, pick the smaller, simpler designs. If they love a joke, pick the one with the club-badge humor. Our Garden Club badge reads like a made-up membership card for a club that was "est. whenever it rained," which tends to suit people who garden for the joy of it and not for the Instagram.

Small gifts that make a bigger set

You can build a gift without spending a lot.

  • A mug plus a packet or two of seeds you know they'll grow.
  • A tote filled with gloves, a notebook and a favorite hand cream.
  • A sweatshirt with a handwritten card about the garden you want to see next year.

Keep the card honest and specific. "I loved the tomatoes you grew last summer" beats any generic note.
