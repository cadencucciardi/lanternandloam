How to Start a Reading Journal: A Simple 10-Minute Setup That Lasts

A reading journal is just a place to keep a record of the books you read and what you thought of them. Most people who start one quit within weeks, usually because the page asked for too much. This guide keeps it small so the habit sticks.

Why keep a reading journal at all?

Ask a few readers and you hear the same reasons:

  • You forget titles. Six months later, "that book with the lighthouse" is all you have.
  • You want to recommend books to friends and actually remember why you liked them.
  • You like seeing the year add up, whether that is 12 books or 52.
  • You want a few lines you loved saved somewhere other than a dog-eared corner.

Pick one of those as your purpose before you buy a notebook or print a page. The purpose decides what you write down.

The six fields that matter

You can track a dozen things. Start with these six and ignore the rest until you want more:

  • Title and author
  • Dates started and finished
  • Format (paper, ebook, audio, library copy)
  • Star rating, out of 5
  • One favorite line or moment
  • One sentence on how it felt ("slow start, great ending")

That is a five-minute entry. If a book was not for you, write "did not finish" with the page you stopped on and move on. No guilt required.

Three easy setups

1. The minimal log. One line per book: title, author, rating. Good if you only want a record. Ten seconds per book.

2. The mood journal. Add the six fields above plus one line on where you read it and what you were drinking. Good if you love the cozy ritual of reading and want to remember the season a book belonged to.

3. The yearly challenge. Set a number, then fill in a tracker as you finish each book. Be realistic: 20 books a year is about one every 2.5 weeks, and 52 means a book a week. Choosing a number you can hit beats a number that looks impressive.

A sample filled-in entry

Here is what a real, low-effort entry looks like:

  • Title: *A made-up example, The Lamp on Orchard Lane*
  • Dates: Nov 2 to Nov 9, paper copy
  • Rating: 4 of 5
  • Favorite line: the one about the kettle going cold while she read the letter
  • In a sentence: Gentle, a bit predictable, perfect with tea

Notice what is missing: no summary, no essay. A summary is what the back cover is for.

A 10-minute plan to start today

  • Minutes 1-2: Choose your purpose and your setup from above.
  • Minutes 3-5: Get your pages ready. Use any notebook, or print a ready-made set.
  • Minutes 6-8: Log the book you are reading right now, even if you are halfway through.
  • Minutes 9-10: Pick a fixed spot: the same chair, the same lamp, or beside your bookmark. Put the journal where you will see it.

The trick is to write the entry right when you close the book, while the ending is fresh.

Tips so you do not quit by February

  • Keep the journal next to where you read, not on a shelf.
  • Allow messy pages. Crossed-out ratings are fine.
  • Skip a month and just restart. Do not go back and fill gaps unless you want to.
  • Add one extra field only after you have kept up the basics for a month.
  • Do not log everything. Skipping a book you barely remember is allowed.

Start small

The best reading journal is the one you actually open. Pick your six fields, log the book on your nightstand, and add a little each time you finish one. By next winter you will have a record you will enjoy rereading.
