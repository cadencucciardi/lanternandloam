Most gift swaps don't go wrong because of the game. They go wrong because nobody decided the budget, the date or who is bringing what until the day before. This checklist is for whoever got volunteered to organize: the office manager, the room parent, the aunt who always hosts.

It covers the planning side of a party. Two to three weeks before: decide the basics

  • Pick the date, time and place, and send one clear invitation. Ask for replies by a set date.
  • Choose the swap type: white elephant (gifts are opened and can be stolen) or Secret Santa (everyone draws a name). Groups with people who barely know each other usually find white elephant easier.
  • Set one budget and say it in the invitation. A single number such as $20 avoids the awkward gift that is far above or below the rest.
  • Ask about allergies and dietary needs now if food is involved.
  • Decide who brings what, and write it down in one place so you don't end up with five desserts and no drinks.

One week before: confirm and prepare

  • Send a short reminder with the time, address and budget again.
  • Count your final guests, then set the number of gifts. In a white elephant, one gift per person keeps it fair.
  • Decide the house rules and print them, or write them on a card. Common ones: how many times a gift can be stolen, and what happens with a late arrival.
  • Buy basic supplies: tape, pens, a bowl for drawing numbers or names, trash bags for wrapping paper.
  • Plan the gift table. Gifts look better and are easier to keep track of in one spot.

Three days before: wrap and label

Labeling is the part people forget. Even in a white elephant, a tag helps: a "To" line for a Secret Santa recipient, or a "From" line you can fill in after the reveal.

Party day: run it smoothly

  • Set up the gift table and a clear spot for the person running the game.
  • Put the budget and the rules where everyone can see them.
  • Start the swap after people have had a snack. A hungry group is a loud group.
  • Keep a short list of the order in which people open gifts, so no one gets skipped.
  • Take a photo of the pile before it turns into wrapping paper.

Office-specific reminders

  • Make participation optional. Some people can't or don't want to spend money, and a quiet opt-out protects morale.
  • Keep gifts work-appropriate and set a rule for it.
  • Plan for remote or absent coworkers: you can mail a gift in advance or give a gift card instead.
  • Check whether your workplace has any rules about gifts or alcohol.

Family-specific reminders

  • Group kids and adults separately, or use a lower budget for kids.
  • Add a no-pressure option for guests who would rather skip the stealing: a draw-a-name swap works well.
  • Think about travel. Gifts that fit in a bag are easier for guests who flew in.
  • Have one or two spare gifts wrapped in case someone shows up unexpectedly.

After the party

  • Write down what worked while it's fresh: the best price point, the best game rule, the dish that disappeared first.
  • Send a short thank-you note to the people who helped. A printed card or message is enough.
  • Save your checklist for next year so you only have to adjust it.
