A Cozy New Year Reset Week: Home, Money and Books With Printables You Can Print Tonight

This guide features products from our own shop, Lantern & Loam. Our designs are created with the help of AI design tools and printed to order by our production partner, Printify.

Most New Year resets fail because they try to fix everything on January 1st. This one is smaller on purpose. You pick one short job per day for a week, print what helps, and stop when it's done. Nothing here needs special supplies, just a home printer, a pen and a cup of something warm.

Why a reset week works better than a resolution

A resolution is one big promise. A reset week is seven small, finishable tasks. Finishing things is what builds the habit, so aim for twenty to thirty minutes a day, not a heroic weekend.

Printables help because they remove the "where do I even start" step. You print a page, you fill it in, and the plan is on paper instead of in your head. They're instant downloads, so you can print tonight and start tomorrow.

Day 1-2: Reset the home

Start with the space you see most. Clear one surface, run a load of laundry, and take the decorations down only when you're ready (there's no rule about January 2nd).

A weekly schedule keeps the work from piling up again. The Slow Home Reset Cleaning Planner is a printable cleaning schedule built around short, calm sessions rather than a marathon scrub. Print it, tape it inside a cupboard door, and tick things off as you go.

  • Pick one room per day, not the whole house
  • Set a 20-minute timer and stop when it rings
  • Put a donate bag by the door for anything you didn't use this year

Day 3-4: Look at the money without the guilt

January is a good time for a quiet look at your numbers. You don't need a perfect spreadsheet. You need to know what comes in, what has to go out, and what's left.

If your income changes month to month, a planner made for that helps. The Lean Month, Full Month Budget Planner is a printable budget planner aimed at irregular income, so you can plan for both the tight months and the good ones.

  • Write down your fixed bills first
  • Pick one small savings number you can keep up
  • Check in once a week for ten minutes, not daily

Day 5-6: Set a reading habit you'll keep

Reading more is one of the most common goals people set, and one of the easiest to let slide. Make it visible. A simple log makes it feel like a game you can win.

The Lantern Light Reading Journal is a printable book log you can use to track what you read and what you thought of it. Print a few pages, keep them on a clipboard by your favorite chair, and fill one in when you finish a book.

  • Decide a goal that feels easy, then raise it later
  • Log the book the day you finish it
  • Note one line you liked, so you remember it

Day 7: Wrap what's left and plan something kind

If you still have gifts to give, such as a teacher, a neighbor or a friend you saw late, printable tags make plain wrapping look intentional. The Cozy Christmas Gift Tags set has 24 tags in 4 designs. Print on cardstock if you have it, cut them out and punch a hole for twine.

A reset also works as a gift. A printed planner or reading journal with a handwritten note is a thoughtful, low-cost present for someone who has said they want a fresh start. Being instant downloads, they can be printed the same evening.

A simple checklist for the week

  • Day 1-2: one room at a time with the cleaning planner
  • Day 3-4: fixed bills and one savings number in the budget planner
  • Day 5-6: first entry in the reading journal
  • Day 7: wrap anything left and pick one thing to look forward to

A last note

You don't have to finish the whole list. If you only do the cleaning schedule, that's a good week. The goal is a calmer start to the year, and a small plan you actually follow beats a big one you quit.
