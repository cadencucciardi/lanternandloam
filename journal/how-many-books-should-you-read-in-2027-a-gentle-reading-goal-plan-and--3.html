How Many Books Should You Read in 2027? A Gentle Reading Goal Plan (and Three Ways to Track It)

Every January the same question shows up: how many books should I read this year? Most of us grab a big number, feel great for three weeks, then quietly stop counting in March. This guide is a gentler plan. You'll turn a goal into minutes per day, pick one tracking style, and give yourself a kind way back in when you fall behind.

Start with minutes, not books

A book count sounds simple, but books vary a lot. Minutes are easier to plan around. Here is our own rough arithmetic. It assumes an average book of about 90,000 words (a typical novel) and a comfortable reading speed of about 250 words per minute, so roughly 6 hours per book. Your speed and books will differ, so treat it as a starting guess.

  • 6 books in 2027: about 36 hours a year, or around 6 minutes a day. Good for a busy season of life or a return to reading.
  • 12 books: about 72 hours, or around 12 minutes a day. One book a month, and a solid first goal.
  • 24 books: about 144 hours, or around 24 minutes a day. A book every two weeks, easy with a daily habit like a bedtime chapter.
  • 52 books: about 312 hours, or around 51 minutes a day. A book a week is a real commitment, so choose it on purpose.

Audiobooks, short books and re-reads all count if you say they do. It's your goal.

How to choose your number

Look at last year first. If you finished 8 books, aiming for 52 is a wish. Aiming for 10 or 12 is a plan. A useful rule is to take what you actually read and nudge it up by a book or two.

Then ask two questions. Where does reading fit in my day (commute, lunch, bedtime, Sunday morning)? And what would make it feel easy? A fixed spot, a lamp and a mug of tea do more than willpower does.

Three ways to track your reading

You only need one. Pick the one you'll enjoy opening.

  • The simple list. Write the title and the date you finished. That's it. It's fast and works well for a 6 or 12 book goal.
  • The bookshelf fill-in. Draw or print an empty shelf and color in one spine per finished book. It's satisfying to watch it fill, and it works nicely for a visual person or a family goal.
  • The star log. Add a star rating and one line about how the book felt. After a year you have a record of what you actually liked, which makes next year's picks easier.

You can use any of these three styles on plain paper or in a notebook you already own. Set up a tiny monthly check-in

Once a month, spend five minutes with your tracker. Count what you've finished, compare it to your goal divided by 12, and notice how reading felt. You don't need to fix anything. The point is to see where you are before the year gets away from you.

The no-guilt March reset

Almost everyone is behind by March. That's normal, not failure. When it happens:

  • Lower the number without apology. Going from 24 to 15 is still 15 books.
  • Switch to shorter books, short stories or audiobooks for a month.
  • Re-open the habit, not the backlog. Read ten minutes tonight and stop there.
  • Remember that a goal is a tool. If it stops helping, change it.

Your 2027 reading plan in five steps

  • Check how many books you actually finished this year.
  • Choose a number that's one or two above that, and convert it to minutes a day.
  • Pick one tracker: list, bookshelf or star log.
  • Put a five minute check-in on your calendar on the first of each month.
  • Decide now that March is a reset, not a verdict.
