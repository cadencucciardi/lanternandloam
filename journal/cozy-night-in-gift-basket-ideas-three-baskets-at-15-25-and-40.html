Cozy Night In Gift Basket Ideas: Three Baskets at $15, $25 and $40

This guide features products from our own shop, Lantern & Loam. Our designs are created with the help of AI design tools and printed to order by our production partner, Printify.

A cozy night in basket is one of the easiest gifts to get right. It suits a friend, a mother, a teacher, a coworker or a neighbor, and it says "go sit down for an evening" without needing to know anyone's taste in detail. The trouble is deciding what goes in it and what it will cost. So here are three baskets with a budget each. Prices are rough estimates and will vary by store and region.

The basic recipe

Every good night-in basket has the same four parts:

  • Something warm to drink: loose-leaf or bagged tea, cocoa mix, or spiced cider packets.
  • Something to nibble: shortbread, a small bar of chocolate, or a bag of popcorn kernels.
  • Something to do: a paperback, a puzzle book, a deck of cards or a crossword pad.
  • Something soft: wool socks, a small throw, or a pair of fuzzy slippers.

Pick one item from each group and you have a basket. The rest is budget and wrapping.

Basket 1: The $15 Tea and Book Basket

This is the one for coworkers, neighbors and a teacher's last day before break.

  • A small box of tea bags or a loose-leaf sampler (about $5 to $7)
  • A reusable tea infuser if the tea is loose-leaf (about $3)
  • A paperback from a used bookstore or a library sale (about $2 to $4)
  • A bar of chocolate or a few shortbread biscuits (about $2 to $3)

Put it all in a plain bowl, a shoebox wrapped in kraft paper, or a canvas bag. Tie it with string and add a handwritten tag. If you want printed tags, our Cozy Christmas Gift Tags printable has 24 tags in 4 designs you can print at home.

Basket 2: The $25 Cocoa and Puzzle Basket

Good for a family, a houseguest or a friend who has a busy month.

  • Cocoa mix or a jar of drinking chocolate (about $5 to $8)
  • A bag of marshmallows (about $2)
  • A 300 to 500 piece jigsaw puzzle or a puzzle book (about $8 to $12)
  • A pair of warm socks (about $5 to $8)

Tip: tuck the marshmallows in a mason jar with a ribbon so they stay fresh and look like part of the gift. Add a small card that says "one evening, no phones." It costs nothing and it is usually the part people remember.

Use a tote as the wrapping

A canvas tote is a gift wrap that gets reused. Fill it with your basket items and tie the handles together with ribbon. These two fit the theme:

For the person who doesn't drink tea

Not everyone wants a hot drink. Swap the tea for:

  • Sparkling cider or a small bottle of sparkling juice
  • A flavored popcorn kit
  • Hot honey lemon sticks, or a jar of local honey with a wooden spoon
  • A candle and a box of matches (check for scent sensitivities first)

The book and the socks work for anyone.

Wrapping and mailing tips

  • Use shredded paper or tissue to fill gaps. It keeps the items from sliding around.
  • Pack heavy items like puzzles and jars at the bottom.
  • If you are mailing the basket, skip glass and anything that can melt. Choose shelf-stable treats.
  • Put the card on top so it is the first thing they see.
  • Keep a receipt note of what you put in. It is useful if you give the same basket to several people.

Quick shopping checklist

  • Tea or cocoa
  • A treat
  • A book or puzzle
  • Socks or a small throw
  • A container: bowl, tote or mug
  • Tissue, ribbon and a tag
  • A handwritten card

Build your basket one evening, and you will have a thoughtful gift for almost anyone on your list.
