How to Start a Book Club: A First-Meeting Plan, a Voting Method and a Host Rotation

January is when many people decide to read more and ask a friend, "Want to start a book club?" Most clubs fade by spring, usually because nobody planned the second meeting, one person picks every book, or hosting falls on the same two people. This guide gives you a plan for the first night and a system that keeps it going.

1. Five decisions before you send invites

Settle these yourself first, so your invitation sounds clear and not vague.

  • Who: 6 to 10 people is a good start. Not everyone shows up every month, and 5 or 6 usually come.
  • Where: homes in rotation, a cafe, a library room, or video call. Pick one for the first three months.
  • When: a fixed slot, like the first Thursday of the month at 7 pm. A fixed slot beats "whenever we can".
  • What: any genre, or one theme (mysteries, memoir, classics). A theme helps when people disagree about taste.
  • How long: 90 minutes is plenty. Say the end time in the invite.

2. Write an invitation people can answer

Keep it to four lines: what it is, when, where, and what you need from them. For example:

"I'm starting a book club that meets the first Thursday of each month at 7 pm, starting January 7 at my place. First meeting is just getting to know each other and choosing our first three books. Can you come? Reply by December 30."

Ask for a yes or no by a date. Add one line that says no one has to finish every book to be welcome.

3. The 90-minute first meeting

You don't have a book to discuss yet, so the first night is about the club itself. Here is a plan you can follow with a timer.

  • 0:00 to 0:15, arrive and settle in. Snacks, drinks, name tags if people don't know each other.
  • 0:15 to 0:30, round of introductions. Each person shares their name, one book they loved and one they abandoned. The second question gets the best laughs.
  • 0:30 to 0:50, agree on the rules. Use the seven lines below.
  • 0:50 to 1:15, choose the first three books with the voting method in the next section.
  • 1:15 to 1:25, set the host rotation. Fill in the calendar before anyone leaves.
  • 1:25 to 1:30, confirm the next date and take a photo of everyone, if they're happy with that.

4. Pick books without a fight

One person choosing every title is the quickest way to lose a club. Try this three-step vote.

  • Nominate. Everyone writes down one book on a slip of paper. Add a short reason, one line only.
  • Narrow. Read the nominations aloud. Each person gets three tally marks to spread on titles they'd read.
  • Decide. The top three become your next three meetings. The runner-ups go on a "later" list.

A few tips: check page count and whether the library has copies, and let the person who nominated the winning book open that discussion. Keep a notebook of every book you read together. 5. Club rules in seven lines

Read these aloud at the first meeting and change anything people dislike.

  • We meet on the first Thursday, 7 to 8:30 pm.
  • Finishing the book is nice, not required. Come anyway.
  • Spoilers are fine in the room, but flag them in the group chat.
  • If you can't come, tell the host by noon that day.
  • The host picks the spot, provides a drink and keeps time.
  • Everyone gets a turn to talk before anyone gets a second one.
  • Books we don't like are still worth discussing. Be kind about the author and each other.

6. A host rotation that keeps going

Hosting is where clubs quietly die. Write the rotation down on night one.

  • List every member and assign months in order, so no one is asked mid-year.
  • Make hosting light: one drink and something simple to eat, or ask everyone to bring a snack.
  • Let anyone swap with another member if they tell the group a week ahead.
  • The host opens with two questions about the book and closes by checking the next date.
  • Share the calendar in the group chat so everyone sees it.

7. When the club starts to fizzle

Most clubs hit a slump around month three. Try these before giving up.

  • Shorten the books. A 200-page novel is easier than a 600-page epic after a busy month.
  • Change the night or place. A schedule clash is often the actual problem.
  • Reset the list. Run the vote again and let new people nominate.
  • Ask the quiet members what would make it easier to come. Often it's one small change.
  • Skip a month on purpose, rather than letting it drift. Then pick a date and keep it.

Start small

You don't need the perfect book or a big living room. Send the invitation this week, bring a pencil and a plan, and let the first meeting be about choosing what comes next. A club that meets four times is already a success.
