Book Club Gift Ideas: Under $10 to $25, Plus Easy Gift Exchange Rules

This guide features products from our own shop, Lantern & Loam. Our designs are created with the help of AI design tools and printed to order by our production partner, Printify.

A book club is one of the easiest groups to shop for, because everyone already loves the same few things: a good story, a warm drink and a quiet hour to enjoy both. The hard part is the budget. Do you give something small to every member, run a gift swap, or thank the person who hosts every month?

This guide sorts book club gift ideas by price, so you can pick the tier your group is comfortable with. It also covers how to run a gift exchange without awkwardness, and what to do if the meeting is this week and nothing is wrapped.

Under $10: a little something for every member

If your club has 8 to 12 people, a small gift for everyone adds up fast. Keep each one under $10 and make it useful.

  • Bookmarks. Handmade, pressed-flower or plain card ones all work. Slip one into each member's current read.
  • A tea or cocoa sample. A single sachet tied to a tag costs a dollar or two. Read-with-a-warm-mug is the whole point of the evening.
  • A printable reading journal page. Print it at home on nice paper and tuck it in a card. Our Lantern Light Reading Journal is a printable book log members can fill in after each meeting, and you only pay once however many copies you print for the club.
  • Handwritten "best line" cards. Each member gets a card with a favorite quote from a book you read together this year. It costs almost nothing and people keep them.
  • Wrapped with care. Our Cozy Christmas Gift Tags are a printable set, so you can label every little gift tonight.

Up to $25: the book club gift exchange

A gift exchange is the classic year-end club ritual. A $25 cap keeps it fair, and nobody feels they had to overspend.

Ideas that fit the cap:

  • A mug for the reader's morning. The Book Club Is My Happy Place mug or the Book Club Is My Love Language mug pair well with a tea sample and a bookmark.
  • A holiday mug for the book lover. The Merry & Booked mug has a Christmas tree made of stacked books.
  • A keepsake ornament. The Just One More Chapter ornament is a round ceramic ornament with a glowing open book. It is a gift people hang up every year.
  • A cozy sweatshirt. The Merry & Booked sweatshirt suits a swap where the cap is a little higher.
  • A gift basket you assemble. A paperback you loved, a tea bag, a candle and a handwritten note. Build it around one person's tastes.

How to run the swap (in four steps)

1. Set the cap early. Announce the dollar limit when you send the date, not at the meeting. 2. Draw names. Write each name on a slip, fold it and pass a bowl around, or draw by message a few weeks ahead so people have time to shop. 3. Give a clue. Ask each member for two or three likes, such as favorite genre, drink and color. It stops the gift-card default. 4. Wrap in a book theme. Brown paper, twine and a bookmark make every gift look like it belongs together.

If a member would rather not shop, a "gift of a book you loved" with a handwritten note is a lovely swap option within the cap.

Thank-you gifts for the host

Whoever hosts regularly gives up their living room, their snacks and their evening. A thank-you does not need to be big.

  • A mug and tea set for their next meeting. The mugs above work well.
  • A shared card with a line from each member.
  • A tote for the host to carry the club's books.
  • A promise to bring the snacks next time. Honestly, this is often the best one.

Last-minute: print tonight, give tomorrow

If your meeting is tomorrow and you have not bought anything, printables save the day. They download instantly and print on any home printer.

  • Print the Lantern Light Reading Journal pages and clip them with a pencil and a tea bag.
  • Print the Cozy Christmas Gift Tags and add one to each small gift.
  • Write a "best read of the year" card for each member. Name the book that suited them and why.

A printable is not a replacement for a gift you can hold, but combined with a tea bag and a good bookmark, it looks thoughtful and costs little.

A year-end club ritual worth keeping

At your December meeting, have everyone name their best read of the year in one sentence. Write the list on a card, then copy it for everyone. In January, it becomes the starting point for next year's reading list, and a little keepsake too.
